Film or coating?

They do different jobs. Film is physical armor against impacts; coating is a chemical shield against the elements. Most clients who plan to keep their car get both.

Physical armor

Paint protection film

Clear, virtually invisible film that takes rock chips, road salt and scratches so your paint doesn't.

  • Partial front, full front or full body
  • Self-healing top layer for light scratches
  • Invisible on any color, gloss or matte

Paint protection film

Armor for your paint

PPF is a clear urethane film applied to the painted panels that take the most abuse. It absorbs the hits that would otherwise chip or scratch your paint, and it's virtually invisible on any color, in gloss or matte.

Every pattern is fitted to your specific vehicle, with edges placed and wrapped to keep the factory look.

What it stops

  • Rock chips from highway driving
  • Road salt, sand and debris
  • Light scratches and swirl marks
  • Bug acids, bird droppings and tree sap

Where it goes

  • Front bumper, hood and fenders
  • Mirrors and headlights
  • Rocker panels and door edges
  • Or every painted panel, with full body

Ceramic coating

Gloss that's easy to keep

A ceramic coating is a liquid layer that bonds to your paint and hardens into a slick, glossy shield. Water beads and rolls off, dirt has less to grip, and washing takes a fraction of the effort.

We're honest about what it does and doesn't do, so you know exactly what you're paying for.

What it does

  • Deep, glossy finish
  • Water and grime bead off and rinse away
  • Protects against UV fading and oxidation
  • Resists bird droppings, bug splatter and sap

What it doesn't

  • Stop rock chips (that's PPF)
  • Make the car scratch-proof
  • Replace regular washing
  • Hide swirls without polishing first

Which one is right for you?

A quick guide. We'll confirm in person after we see the car and hear how you drive it.

Choose

Ceramic coating

You mostly drive around town, want a deep gloss, and want washing to be quick and easy.

Choose

Paint protection film

You spend time on the highway, your car is new or freshly painted, or you've already seen chips on the front end.

Choose

Both

You plan to keep the car, want it to stay as close to new as possible, and want the easiest upkeep. Film on the front, coating over everything.

How we protect your car

Film and coating both lock in whatever is underneath, so the paint has to be right before anything goes on.

  1. Evaluation

    We look over your paint with you, talk through how you drive, and recommend the right package with an exact quote.

  2. Wash & decontamination

    A full hand wash, then iron and clay decontamination to pull out bonded contaminants the wash leaves behind.

  3. Polish

    For Level II and III coatings, the paint is polished to bring out gloss and clarity before it's sealed in.

  4. Film installation

    For PPF, each panel is fitted with a pattern made for your vehicle, with edges placed and wrapped for a clean finish.

  5. Coating application

    The coating is applied panel by panel in a controlled space, leveled by hand and given time to cure.

  6. Inspection & handover

    We inspect the finish in good light and walk you through care, including how long to wait before the first wash.

Caring for protected paint

Film and coatings make upkeep easier, not unnecessary. A few habits keep them performing for as long as they're rated.

Do

  • Hand wash with a pH-neutral soap
  • Use clean microfiber towels to dry
  • Rinse off bird droppings and sap quickly
  • Follow the first-wash wait time we give you

Avoid

  • Automatic brush car washes
  • Harsh, high-alkaline cleaners
  • Pressure washing close to film edges
  • Abrasive polishes and compounds

Protection questions

What's the difference between PPF and ceramic coating?

Paint protection film is a clear, physical layer that absorbs rock chips, scratches and road debris. Ceramic coating is a thin, hard chemical layer that adds gloss, repels water and grime, and makes the car much easier to wash. Film protects against impacts; coating protects against the elements and makes upkeep easier.

Do I need both?

Not always. If your main concern is rock chips on the front end, PPF is the priority. If you want gloss and easier washing, ceramic coating is the place to start. Many owners who plan to keep their car do both: film on the high-impact areas and coating over the whole car, including the film.

Can you put ceramic coating over PPF or a wrap?

Yes. Ceramic coating bonds to paint, wrap film and PPF. Coating over film adds gloss and makes the film easier to keep clean.

Will I be able to see the film?

PPF is virtually invisible on any color. Up close, you may notice an edge where a partial package ends, so we place edges where they're least visible and wrap them around panel edges where the panel allows.

Does ceramic coating stop rock chips and scratches?

No. A coating adds some resistance to light marring, but it's a thin layer and won't stop rock chips or deeper scratches. That's what PPF is for, which is why the two are often paired.

Do I still need to wash my car?

Yes, but it's much easier. Dirt, water and grime release more easily from a coated or filmed surface, so washes are quicker and the car stays clean longer. Hand washing with a pH-neutral soap is best.

Is the interior detail really included?

Yes. A complimentary express interior detail is included with Level II and Level III ceramic coatings.

Can I pay over time?

Yes. You can split the cost of larger jobs into smaller payments with Klarna or Afterpay. Ask about it when you get your estimate.
