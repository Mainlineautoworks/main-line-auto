Why tint your windows

Tint does a lot more than darken the glass. Here's what you get every time you get in the car.

01

A cooler cabin

Film rejects solar heat, so the car heats up less in the sun and the A/C doesn't have to work as hard.

02

UV protection

Quality films block most of the sun's UV rays, protecting your skin on long drives and helping prevent interior fading and cracking.

03

Less glare

Softer light from the sun and headlights makes driving more comfortable, especially early morning and late afternoon.

04

Privacy & looks

Keep belongings out of sight and give the car a clean, finished look that pairs with any color or wrap.

Know before you go dark

Pennsylvania tint law

Pennsylvania's tint rules are stricter than in most states, and they depend on the type of vehicle. The limit applies to the glass and film together, and factory glass already blocks some light, so we check your windows before we recommend a shade.

This is a general guide, not legal advice. Rules can change, and PennDOT also offers a medical exemption process. Ask us about your vehicle.

How we tint

Clean glass and careful edges are what separate a great tint job from one that peels.

  1. Consultation

    We talk through the look you want, compare shades and films, and confirm what's legal for each window on your vehicle.

  2. Glass prep

    Every window is deep-cleaned inside, with door seals and edges cleared, so no dust or debris gets trapped under the film.

  3. Cut to fit

    Film is cut to the exact shape of each window, with edges finished cleanly to the glass.

  4. Heat shaping

    Curved glass, like most rear windows, is heat-shrunk so the film lies perfectly flat without creases.

  5. Installation

    The film is applied from the inside and squeegeed firmly to push out water and air for a clear, even finish.

  6. Final check

    We inspect every window in good light and go over curing and care before you head out.

After your tint

Film takes a few days to fully cure. A little haze or a few small water spots during that time are normal and clear up on their own.

Do

  • Keep windows up for the first few days
  • Clean with an ammonia-free glass cleaner
  • Use a soft microfiber cloth
  • Let the sun help it cure faster

Avoid

  • Ammonia-based cleaners
  • Scrapers, razors or rough sponges on the film
  • Picking at the edges
  • Stick-on suction cups or decals on tinted glass

Tint questions

Is window tint legal in Pennsylvania?

Yes, within limits. On sedans, coupes and other passenger cars, the side windows and rear window must let more than 70% of light through. On SUVs, vans and pickup trucks, the front side windows have the same 70% rule, but the rear side windows and back glass can be any darkness. On every vehicle, the windshield can only be tinted along the top strip, above the AS-1 line. Mirrored or reflective film isn't allowed.

How dark can I go?

It depends on the vehicle and the window. Pennsylvania measures the glass and film together, and factory glass already blocks some light, so we'll check your windows and tell you what's legal before we install anything. On SUVs, vans and trucks, the rear windows can go as dark as you like.

What's the difference between ceramic and standard tint?

Ceramic film uses ceramic particles to block heat, so it keeps the cabin noticeably cooler than standard film at the same shade. That means you can choose a lighter look and still get strong heat rejection. Standard film gives you the darker look, privacy and glare reduction at a lower price.

Can I get a medical exemption for darker tint?

PennDOT has a medical exemption process for drivers with certain conditions, certified by a doctor. Contact PennDOT for the details, and let us know if you have an exemption so we can plan your install.

How soon can I roll my windows down?

Wait a few days so the film can fully bond to the glass. We'll tell you exactly how long for the film and the weather when you pick up the car.

Is haze or small water spots after installation normal?

Yes. A little haze or a few small water pockets are normal for the first days while the film cures. They clear up on their own as the moisture evaporates.

Will my tint turn purple or bubble?

Quality films are built to keep their color and stay bonded. Purple, bubbling tint is usually old or low-grade dyed film. We'll help you choose a film that's made to last.

Can I pay over time?

Yes. You can split the cost of larger jobs into smaller payments with Klarna or Afterpay. Ask about it when you get your estimate.
