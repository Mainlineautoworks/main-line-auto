How we paint calipers

A finish that lasts comes from the prep. Brake dust, grease and rust all have to go before any color goes on.

  1. Choose your color

    We look at your car and wheels together, try colors, and talk through decals if you want them.

  2. Wheels off

    The wheels come off so we can reach every visible side of each caliper.

  3. Deep clean

    Brake dust, grease and loose rust are removed, and the caliper is degreased so the coating bonds properly.

  4. Protect the brakes

    Rotors, pads, brake lines, seals and bleeder screws are carefully protected. Only the caliper body gets painted.

  5. Color coats

    The high-temperature coating goes on in even coats for full, smooth coverage, with decals added if you chose them.

  6. Reassemble & check

    Once the finish has set, the wheels go back on, lug nuts are torqued, and we inspect every caliper with you.

Keeping them bright

The coating is made for brake heat, salt and dust. A little care when you wash the wheels keeps the color looking fresh for years.

Do

  • Use a pH-neutral wheel cleaner
  • Clean with a soft wheel brush
  • Rinse off brake dust and road salt regularly, especially in winter

Avoid

  • Harsh acid-based wheel cleaners
  • Wire or stiff-bristle brushes
  • Letting heavy brake dust sit for weeks

Caliper questions

Does painting calipers affect braking?

No. Only the outside of the caliper body is painted. The rotors, pads, brake lines, seals and bleeder screws are protected during the work, so nothing that affects how your brakes work is coated.

Will the paint hold up to brake heat?

Yes. We use a pro-grade, high-temperature coating made for brake components, so it won't fade or discolor from normal brake heat, salt or brake dust.

Can you match my car's color or my wrap?

Yes. We can match your factory paint, your wrap or your trim, or pick a contrasting color that stands out. Use the color previewer to try ideas, and we'll confirm the color with you before we start.

Should I paint all four calipers or just the fronts?

Most people do all four, because the rear calipers are visible through most wheel designs and mismatched colors stand out. Fronts only is possible if your rear brakes are hard to see.

Can you add a logo or lettering to my calipers?

Yes. Custom caliper decals are available, from simple lettering to a logo. Tell us what you have in mind and we'll show you options.

How long will my car be at the shop?

It depends on the number of calipers, their condition and whether you're adding decals. We'll give you a timeline with your estimate, and we can pick up and drop off the car.

How do I clean painted calipers?

Use a pH-neutral wheel cleaner and a soft brush. Avoid harsh acid-based wheel cleaners and stiff wire brushes. Regular cleaning keeps brake dust from building up on the finish.
