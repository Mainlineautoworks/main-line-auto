Choose your finish

The finish changes the character of a color as much as the color itself. Here's how each one looks on a car.

Gloss

Deep, wet shine like fresh paint. The most popular choice, and the easiest to keep clean.

Satin

A soft, low sheen that shows off a car's body lines without hard reflections. Modern and understated.

Matte

Flat, no shine at all. A bold, stealthy look that needs a little more care when washing.

Metallic

Fine metal flake that sparkles in the sun, like a factory metallic paint.

Color-shift

Changes color as you walk around the car or the light moves. Impossible to ignore.

Carbon fiber

Textured weave for roofs, hoods, mirrors and trim. A performance look without the cost of real carbon.

How a wrap comes together

Most of a great wrap is the work you never see: the prep, the disassembly and the edges.

  1. Consultation

    We talk through the look you're after, compare film samples against your car, and give you a clear price and timeline.

  2. Paint inspection

    We check the paint for chips, repaints and clear-coat issues that could show through or be affected, and tell you before we start.

  3. Deep prep

    A full wash, paint decontamination and clay, then a panel wipe to remove wax and oils so the film bonds properly.

  4. Careful disassembly

    Where it makes for a cleaner job, we remove parts like handles, badges and lights so the film can wrap around edges instead of stopping short.

  5. Installation

    Film is laid panel by panel, worked into recesses and trimmed by hand. Edges are post-heated so they stay put.

  6. Final inspection

    We reassemble, inspect every panel and edge in good light, and walk you through the car and how to care for it.

Making it last

Quality cast films typically last several years. Sun exposure, the finish you choose and how the car is washed make the biggest difference. Roofs and hoods face the sun most and usually age first.

Want it even easier to clean? Ask about adding a ceramic coating made for vinyl over your wrap.

Do

  • Hand wash with a pH-neutral soap
  • Clean off bird droppings, sap and fuel quickly
  • Park in a garage or shade when you can
  • Use products made for vinyl, especially on matte and satin

Avoid

  • Automatic brush car washes
  • Pressure washing close to the film's edges
  • Waxes and polishes on matte or satin finishes
  • Scraping ice or snow off wrapped panels

Wrap questions

Does a wrap damage my paint?

Not on healthy factory paint. Quality wrap film uses an adhesive designed to come off cleanly, and the film shields the paint from sun and light scratches while it's on. Repainted panels, peeling clear coat or deep chips are the exceptions, so we inspect the paint first and tell you about anything that could be affected.

How long does a car wrap last?

Quality cast films typically last several years. How long yours lasts depends on the film, the finish, how much time the car spends in the sun and how it's washed. Roofs and hoods take the most sun and usually age first. We'll tell you what to expect from the film you choose.

How much does a wrap cost?

It depends on the size of the vehicle, how complex the body panels are, the film and finish you choose, and how much is being wrapped. A roof or mirror wrap costs a fraction of a full color change. Estimates are free.

How long will my car be at the shop?

Partial wraps and chrome deletes are much quicker than a full color change. We'll give you a timeline with your estimate, and we can pick up and drop off the car.

Can I wrap a leased car?

Usually, yes. Because a wrap comes off, many lessees wrap their cars and have the film removed before turn-in. Check your lease terms first, and let us know so we can plan for removal.

Will rock chips or scratches show through the wrap?

Vinyl is thin and follows the surface under it, so deep chips, dents and rough spots can show through. We'll point out anything we see during inspection and talk through your options before we start.

How do I wash a wrapped car?

Hand washing with a pH-neutral soap is best. Avoid automatic brush washes, keep a pressure washer at a distance and away from the film's edges, and clean off bird droppings and fuel spills quickly. We'll send you home with care instructions for your finish.

Can I pay for my wrap over time?

Yes. You can split the cost of larger jobs into smaller payments with Klarna or Afterpay. Ask about it when you get your estimate.
